# ⭐ 16. What is GAN?

**GAN = Generative Adversarial Network**

> GAN is a generative model consisting of two neural networks, a **Generator** and a **Discriminator**, which compete with each other during training.

```text
             Random Noise
                  ↓
             GENERATOR
                  ↓
             Fake Image
                  ↓
           DISCRIMINATOR
             ↙       ↘
          Real       Fake
```

### Generator

Creates fake/synthetic data.

### Discriminator

Attempts to distinguish real data from generated data.

---

# ⭐ 17. Why is GAN called "Adversarial"?

> Because the Generator and Discriminator have competing objectives. The Generator tries to fool the Discriminator, while the Discriminator tries to correctly identify generated data.

---

# 18. What is the goal of a Generator?

> To generate realistic samples that resemble real training data and eventually fool the Discriminator.

---

# 19. What is the goal of a Discriminator?

> To distinguish between real samples and generated samples.

---

# 20. What are applications of GAN?

> Image generation, face generation, image-to-image translation, super-resolution, style transfer, synthetic data generation, and creative applications.

---

# 21. What are limitations of GAN?

Important:

- Training instability
- Mode collapse
- Difficult convergence
- Sensitivity to hyperparameters
- Generator/discriminator imbalance

---

# ⭐ 22. What is Mode Collapse?

> Mode collapse occurs when a GAN Generator produces limited varieties of outputs instead of generating diverse samples.

Example:

You ask the GAN to generate 1,000 different faces but it keeps producing very similar faces.

---

# ⭐ 23. What is VAE?

**VAE = Variational Autoencoder**

> VAE is a generative model based on an encoder-decoder architecture that learns a probabilistic latent representation and can generate new samples from that latent space.

```text
Input
 ↓
Encoder
 ↓
Latent Space
 ↓
Decoder
 ↓
Generated Output
```

---

# 24. What is an Autoencoder?

> An Autoencoder is a neural network that learns to encode input data into a compact representation and then reconstruct the original data.

```text
Input
 ↓
Encoder
 ↓
Latent Representation
 ↓
Decoder
 ↓
Reconstructed Output
```

---

# 25. Autoencoder vs VAE

| Autoencoder | VAE |
|---|---|
| Learns representation | Learns probabilistic representation |
| Mainly reconstruction | Reconstruction + generative sampling |
| Latent space need not be structured probabilistically | Structured probabilistic latent space |

---

# ⭐ 26. What is CycleGAN?

> CycleGAN is a GAN-based image-to-image translation model that can learn to translate images between two domains without requiring paired training examples.

Example:

```text
Horse → Zebra
Zebra → Horse
```

or:

```text
Summer → Winter
```

---

# 27. What is a Discriminative Model?

> A discriminative model learns to distinguish between classes or predict an output from input data.

Example:

```text
Image → Cat / Dog
```

---

# ⭐ 28. Generative vs Discriminative Model

| Generative | Discriminative |
|---|---|
| Generates new data | Predicts/classifies |
| Learns data distribution | Learns decision boundary/conditional relationship |
| GAN, VAE, Diffusion | SVM, Logistic Regression |
| Text → Image | Image → Cat/Dog |

# ⭐ UNIT IV — Deep Dive into GANs

## 57. Explain GAN architecture.

```text
                 Random Noise
                      ↓
                 Generator
                      ↓
                Fake Sample
                      ↓
              Discriminator
               ↙          ↘
            Real          Fake
```

The Generator tries to produce realistic samples while the Discriminator tries to distinguish real from fake.

---

# 58. How is a GAN trained?

Simple answer:

> The Generator creates fake samples. The Discriminator receives both real and generated samples and learns to distinguish them. The Generator is then updated to make its outputs more realistic. This process continues iteratively.

---

# 59. What are challenges in GAN training?

> Mode collapse, unstable training, difficult convergence, and imbalance between Generator and Discriminator.

---

# 60. What is the future of GANs?

> GANs remain useful for image synthesis, style transfer, synthetic data and other generation tasks, although diffusion models have become very important for many modern image-generation applications.
